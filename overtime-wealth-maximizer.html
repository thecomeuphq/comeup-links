Straight Answers

Is overtime taxed more than regular pay?

No — overtime isn't taxed at a special higher rate. Your OT dollars are taxed at the same marginal bracket as any other income. The catch is perception: because OT stacks on top of your regular pay, those extra dollars land entirely in your highest bracket, and payroll withholding can look aggressive on a big check.

If too much gets withheld, it comes back to you as a tax refund. The money isn't gone — but budgeting off the gross number will wreck your month, which is exactly why Step 2 exists.

What's the burnout trap?

The trap is treating overtime as infinite. You volunteer for every shift, the money quietly absorbs into lifestyle spending, and six months later you're exhausted, your base-pay budget still doesn't work, and quitting the OT feels like a pay cut you can't afford.

The safeguard is a finish line. Step 4 turns each goal into a specific number of hours — when the hours are worked and the target is hit, the extra shifts stop. Overtime is a tool with an off switch, not a second job you can never leave.

Why does the reward bucket matter? Shouldn't it all go to debt?

All-debt plans fail the same way crash diets do: total deprivation, then one bad week, then the whole plan is abandoned. A small, planned reward — 15% is plenty — makes the system survivable for the months a real payoff actually takes.

It's also honest accounting. You earned that money at 3 a.m. on a warehouse floor. Sending a deliberate slice of it to something you enjoy isn't weakness; it's the maintenance cost of a plan you'll still be running in month six.
