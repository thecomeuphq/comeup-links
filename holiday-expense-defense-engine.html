        Straight Answers

        Why do the holidays cause so much January debt?

        Because the spending is concentrated but the income isn't. Most people fund five or six weeks of gifts, food, and travel out of one or two regular paychecks, so the overflow lands on credit cards and buy-now-pay-later plans — then January's minimum payments, interest, and post-holiday bills all hit at once. The fix isn't spending less overall; it's spreading the same total across every paycheck from now until the event, which is exactly what the siphon does.

        How does the per-person cap trick work?

        You decide the number before the season starts — say $75 per person — and that number, not the store display or a last-minute guilt trip, decides the gift. Caps kill the two biggest budget leaks: upgrading "just a little" for each person, and panic-buying extras in the final week. Write the cap next to each name in Step 2, and when a gift hits the cap, you're done with that person. Done is a good feeling.

        What if the event is only 3 weeks away?

        Then the math gets blunt: fewer paychecks means a bigger siphon from each one, and the stress check will probably flag it. You have three honest levers — trim the per-person caps until the siphon fits under 10% of your net pay, shrink the guest or gift list, or split the difference and cash-fund most of it instead of all of it. What doesn't work is charging it and hoping January-you earns more. Short runway is exactly when the caps matter most.
