    Straight Answers

    What is quarterly estimated tax?

    When no employer withholds tax from your income, the IRS wants it paid as you earn — in four installments due January 15, April 15, June 15, and September 15. You file Form 1040-ES, pay online or by mail, and then square up on your annual return. Skip the quarters and you can owe an underpayment penalty on top of the tax itself, even if you pay in full in April.

    What's the "April shock"?

    It's the moment a gig or hybrid worker files their first return and discovers a full year of untaxed side income at once — self-employment tax plus income tax, all due on one day, sometimes with a penalty for not paying quarterly. A $1,200/month side hustle with no reserves can mean a $4,000+ bill out of nowhere. The Shield Card's reserve rate exists so April feels like a formality, not an emergency.

    How does the mileage deduction actually work?

    For self-employment driving — deliveries, rideshare, trips to clients — you can deduct the IRS standard mileage rate ($0.70 per mile for 2025) instead of tracking actual gas and repairs. At 600 business miles a month, that's $420 a month straight off your taxable income. The catch: you need a mileage log with dates, miles, and purpose. A notebook or a tracking tool works — reconstructing it from memory in April doesn't.
