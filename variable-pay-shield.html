Straight Answers

Why are the high weeks the dangerous ones?

Because a $1,200 week feels like a raise. You spend like the money is permanent — bigger grocery run, new shoes, catching up on fun — and then the $400 week lands with rent due. The high week isn't the reward; it's the funding source for the low week. The shield works because it taxes the fat check before your lifestyle can.

How big should my Valley Buffer be?

Start with two full pay periods at your Bare-Bones Floor — that's the target this page calculates. It covers the most common disaster: two bad periods back to back. Once you hold that line comfortably, grow it toward one full month of floor expenses, then graduate the overflow into a real emergency fund.

What counts as the Bare-Bones Floor?

Only what keeps you housed, fed, mobile, and out of default: rent, utilities, minimum debt payments, basic groceries, and gas to get to work. If a bill can be paused, cut, or skipped for two weeks without real damage, it doesn't belong in the floor — a lower floor means a smaller buffer to build and a plan you'll actually stick to.

Where do I actually keep the Valley Buffer?

In a separate savings account — ideally at a different bank or in a separate "vault" your spending can't touch by accident. Same account as your everyday money means it will quietly get spent. Out of sight, one transfer away, is the whole trick.
