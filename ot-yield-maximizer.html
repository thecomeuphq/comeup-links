Straight answers

Why does a big OT week sometimes barely move my check?

Because overtime is taxed at your marginal rate, not your average rate. Your regular pay fills up the cheap tax brackets first, so every OT dollar lands in the highest bracket you've reached — plus 7.65% FICA on top. A fat OT week can also push your annualized income into the next bracket on that check's withholding math. The hours still pay; they just pay less per hour than the 1.5× on the board suggests. That gap is exactly what the Real Net Hourly Yield above measures.

How do shift differentials work?

A differential is an extra amount per hour — say $1.50/hr — paid on top of your base rate for nights, weekends, or hard-to-fill shifts. It usually applies to every hour on that shift, including overtime hours, and at many employers the OT premium is calculated on your rate including the differential. This tool adds the differential on your straight-time hours to your baseline and counts it on premium hours too, so check your own pay policy if your numbers look slightly different.

What does the 65% Burnout Alert actually mean?

It compares what each premium hour pays after tax (your Real Net Hourly Yield) against what that hour pays before tax (its gross premium rate). If you're keeping less than 65% of the gross rate, taxes are eating more than a third of every extra hour — the point where a lot of workers find the missed sleep, missed games, and missed rest stop feeling worth it. It doesn't mean "never take OT." It means the routing matters more: aim those dollars at high-interest debt first, where every dollar also kills future interest.

Is the Overtime Efficiency Rating my credit score or something official?

No — it's a score this tool calculates for this paycheck only. It measures how efficiently your premium hours converted into spendable cash after estimated taxes. Higher means your OT is working hard for you; lower means bracket creep and withholding are taking a bigger cut, and it's worth looking at your W-4 or your routing split.

Tax figures on this page are estimates built from 2025 federal brackets, the standard deduction, FICA at 7.65%, and a typical effective state rate. They do not account for pre-tax deductions (401(k), health premiums), local taxes, garnishments, or your exact W-4 elections, and they are not tax advice. For decisions that matter, compare against your real pay stub or talk to a tax professional.
