Straight answers

The questions every trade worker asks before the first breakdown — not after.

Why is tool-truck credit a trap?

Because it's sold at your lowest moment — tool dead, shift starting, truck right there. Weekly-payment plans on the big tool trucks commonly run 20% APR and up, stretch a $400 tool into $550+, and the payment follows you even if the job doesn't. Worse, it trains the habit: every breakdown becomes debt instead of a withdrawal from a reserve you already funded. A $12–$25 per-paycheck set-aside costs less per month than one tool-truck payment — and when the tool breaks, you pay cash and keep working.

What counts as deductible work gear?

If you're 1099 / self-employed: tools, safety boots, PPE, tool storage, repairs, and required certification or license fees are generally deductible business expenses on Schedule C — keep receipts and a log. If you're W-2: the federal deduction for unreimbursed employee expenses has been suspended since 2018, so your best moves are employer reimbursements (tax-free to you under an accountable plan) and tool allowances — plus a handful of states still allow the deduction on the state return. Either way, documented gear spending is money on the table; undocumented spending is just gone.

How often should I reassess my gear defense?

Run this check every 6 months, and immediately after three events: a job or trade change (new required kit), a theft or major breakage (your risk flags just changed), and any raise or schedule change (your per-paycheck number can move). Boots and PPE deserve a hard look at the 6-month mark regardless — they're the gear most likely to fail mid-shift and the cheapest to keep ahead of.
