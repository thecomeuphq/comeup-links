FAQ

Straight Answers

Does overtime push me into a higher tax bracket?
No. Tax brackets are marginal: only the dollars above a bracket line are taxed at the higher rate — never your whole check. What actually happens on a blitz week is a withholding spike: payroll annualizes that one big check and withholds as if you earned that pace all year. Your real annual tax is figured on your real annual income when you file.
Will I get the extra withholding back at tax time?
Yes — if you over-withheld, it comes back as part of your refund (or reduces what you owe). Withholding is a prepayment, not a final bill. The catch is timing: the IRS holds that money interest-free for months. The pre-tax shield in Tier 1 keeps more of it in your paycheck — and in your 401(k) — right now instead of next spring.
Should I change my W-4 for one overtime blitz?
Usually no. Rewriting your W-4 for a few heavy weeks risks under-withholding the rest of the year, and many payroll departments process W-4 changes slowly. A temporary pre-tax contribution bump is the cleaner lever: it lowers the taxable wages payroll sees during the blitz, then you revert it when the schedule ends — no changes to your baseline withholding setup.
Does a 401(k) contribution dodge FICA too?
No. 401(k) deferrals skip federal and (in most states) state income tax, but Social Security and Medicare still apply. An HSA contribution made through your employer's cafeteria plan is the exception — it skips income tax and FICA. Either way, this engine's shield math uses your combined federal + state marginal rate, so the estimate stays honest.

How the estimate works: weekly paychecks; OT at 1.5× past 40 hours; federal withholding simulated with a simplified IRS percentage method using estimated 2026 brackets and standard deductions (Single $16,100 · MFJ $32,200 · Head of Household $24,150); FICA at a flat 7.65%; state/local at the rate you enter. Real paychecks vary with pay frequency, other deductions, credits, and employer payroll settings.
