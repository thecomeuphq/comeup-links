Emergency fund basics

What counts as an emergency fund?

Cash you can reach today — a regular savings account, not investments, not a credit line, not money tied up somewhere. It's there for true emergencies: a job loss, a car repair that strands you, a medical bill, a busted water heater. Vacations, sales, and "good deals" don't count.

Why isn't a credit card an emergency fund?

Because a card turns one emergency into two problems: the original bill plus interest on top of it, often at 20%+ APR. Worse, the limit can be cut or the card frozen exactly when you need it most. Cash in savings never charges you interest and never gets declined.

How much is enough?

Start with $1,000 — that covers the most common surprise bills. Then build toward one month of bare-bones survival costs (rent, food, utilities, minimum payments). The long-term target is 3 months of those essentials; if your income is variable or gig-based, aim for 4–6 months.
