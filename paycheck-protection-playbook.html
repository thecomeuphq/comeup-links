Questions

What is a paycheck shield?

It's a one-page plan you build before payday pressure hits. You separate your guaranteed base pay from variable overtime cash, pre-assign your non-negotiable bills and a stash transfer, flag the auto-charges that leak money silently, and decide in advance where extra cash goes. Once it's locked in, every dollar in the cycle already has orders — so a slow week or a surprise draft can't blow up the essentials.

How often should I redo it?

Every pay cycle — it takes about three minutes once you've done it once. Your base pay rarely changes, but overtime, due dates, and BNPL balances shift constantly. Re-running the shield each payday keeps the plan matched to the check you actually got, not the one you hoped for. If nothing changed, just re-print last cycle's split and re-date the signoff.

What if my pay varies wildly week to week?

That's exactly who this is built for. Put your worst realistic week in as base pay — the number you clear even when hours get cut — and treat everything above it as variable cash. Your bills get sized to the floor, not the average, so a bad week never puts rent at risk. Fat weeks just feed the allocation split in Step 4: more to debt, buffer, and flexible spending, in the proportions you already chose.
