      Straight answers

      Can I ask my employer for a paycheck advance?

      Yes — and it's more common than people think. Many employers offer paycheck advances, accrued PTO cash-outs, or hardship grants through HR or payroll, especially for a documented family emergency. Ask in writing, keep it short and factual: what happened, the dates you'll be out, and exactly what you're requesting. The script in Step 4 is written for that conversation. If your company uses an earned-wage-access app (like DailyPay or EarnIn through your employer), that's usually the fastest route — check your employee portal first.

      What does bereavement leave usually cover?

      Most employers give 1–3 paid days for an immediate family member (spouse, child, parent, sibling) and sometimes up to 5; extended family is often 1 day or unpaid. There's no federal law requiring paid bereavement leave for private employers — FMLA leave is unpaid and generally doesn't cover bereavement unless your own health is affected — so your company's handbook is the deciding document. Ask HR for the policy in writing before you assume the days are paid, and confirm whether travel days count.

      How do I protect my rent during a crisis like this?

      Rent is the bill to shield first — a late fee is recoverable, an eviction filing is not. Call your landlord or property manager before the due date, explain there's been a death in the family, and ask for a one-time grace period or split payment in writing. Most will work with a tenant who communicates early and has a payment history. Meanwhile, pause subscriptions and non-essentials (the 60-day freeze in Step 4), and if your state or city has an emergency rental assistance program, apply the same week — those funds are first-come and a short application beats a court date.
