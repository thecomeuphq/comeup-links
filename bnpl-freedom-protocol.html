    Straight answers

    Why does BNPL stack up so fast?

    Because each plan is judged alone. One $25 payment feels like nothing, so you say yes again — and again. But the apps don’t talk to each other, and they all collect on the same two-week rhythm as your paycheck. Four “painless” plans can quietly claim $150–$250 of every check before rent, gas, or groceries get a vote. The stacking is the product working exactly as designed: the smaller each slice feels, the more slices you take.

    What’s the late-fee trap?

    Miss one payment and the “interest-free” deal stops being free. Providers charge late fees (commonly around $7–$10 per miss, depending on the provider and plan), some plans resume interest, and a failed auto-debit can trigger a bank overdraft on top of it. Worst of all, one missed payment doesn’t pause the others — the whole stack keeps drafting. If a due date lands before payday, move money or contact the provider early; silence is the most expensive option.

    Should you ever use BNPL again?

    Only under strict rules — and not until every current plan is dead. If you ever use it again: one plan at a time, only for a true necessity, only if the full price is already sitting in your account, and never stacked against a paycheck that’s already spoken for. If a purchase can’t survive those rules, it isn’t a deal — it’s the trap resetting. Most people who finish this protocol find they don’t miss it.
