Most of the time you do not need every tool. You need the right one. Here are the comparisons people ask for most, written straight.

Snowball vs Avalanche

Which debt payoff method should I use?

Snowball

  • How it works: Pay the smallest balance first, then roll that payment into the next one.
  • Best when: Best when you need to see progress fast. The first balance is gone in weeks, not years.
  • The catch: Costs more interest overall because a high rate may sit untouched longer.
Open Snowball vs Avalanche Calculator

Avalanche

  • How it works: Pay the highest interest rate first, no matter the balance.
  • Best when: Best when the interest rate is brutal and you want the math to win.
  • The catch: The first payoff takes longer, so it can feel like nothing is happening.
Open Debt Strategy Selector

Our take

If the gap in time-to-first-payoff is small, take the snowball and keep the momentum. If one rate is above 25%, take the avalanche. Run both numbers and decide with your own balances, not a rule of thumb.

Debt Free App vs Debt Payoff Calculator

Why should I use one debt tool over the other?

Debt Free App

  • How it works: A saved, ongoing tracker. Add every balance, watch the date move, log payments.
  • Best when: Best when you have many debts and want to keep coming back to the same plan.
  • The catch: Takes a few minutes to set up your debts the first time.
Open Debt Free App

Debt Payoff Calculator

  • How it works: A one-shot calculator. Enter balances and extra payment, get the date and interest.
  • Best when: Best when you want a fast answer right now, with nothing to maintain.
  • The catch: Nothing is saved, so you re-enter it if you want to try a new strategy.
Open Debt Payoff Calculator

Our take

Start with the calculator to get the number, then move to the app when you are ready to actually pay it down. They are built to work together.

Emergency Fund Quiz vs Runway Calculator

Do I need a score, or do I need a date?

Readiness Quiz

  • How it works: Eight questions, scored 0–100, with a verdict on where you stand.
  • Best when: Best when you want a fast honest read on whether you are ready.
  • The catch: It cannot tell you the dollar target.
Open Emergency Fund Readiness Quiz

Runway Calculator

  • How it works: Savings against monthly expenses, output as days and weeks of runway.
  • Best when: Best when you need the actual number — how many days you last.
  • The catch: It needs real expense numbers, which takes a minute to gather.
Open Emergency Fund Runway Calculator

Our take

Take the quiz first for the gut check, then run the calculator for the target. A score without a number does not tell you what to save.

Hourly Money App vs Shift Paycheck Calculator

I only need one pay calculator. Which one?

Hourly Money App

  • How it works: A full app: gross, net, weekly, and a saved log of every shift you work.
  • Best when: Best if you work variable hours and want a history you can look back on.
  • The catch: More setup than a single calculation.
Open Hourly Money App

Shift Paycheck Calculator

  • How it works: One shift in, one number out. Hours, rate, differentials, overtime.
  • Best when: Best for a single shift or a quick what-if before you pick up extra hours.
  • The catch: No history, so it is not a planner.
Open Shift Paycheck Calculator

Our take

If you are trying to decide whether a shift is worth it, use the Shift calculator. If you want to see the year add up, use the app.
