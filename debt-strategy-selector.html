      Quick answers

      Snowball vs. avalanche — what's the actual difference?

      Both methods pay minimums on everything and throw every extra dollar at one target debt. They only differ in how you pick the target.

      Snowball: smallest balance first, regardless of interest rate. You clear whole debts faster, which keeps motivation high — but you may pay more interest overall.

      Avalanche: highest APR first, regardless of balance. It's the mathematically cheapest route and usually the fastest in total dollars — but the first win can take longer if that balance is big.

      Neither is "wrong." The one that wins is the one still running in month six.

      What is a hybrid plan?

      A hybrid plan mixes strategies instead of marrying one. The version recommended here for variable income is minimums plus buffer: every debt gets its minimum every month, any leftover builds (or refills) your cash cushion, and extra payoff payments happen only in strong weeks — aimed at your highest-APR debt first.

      You can also hybrid the payoff order itself: snowball one or two tiny balances for momentum, then switch to avalanche for the expensive debt. The structure bends so a lean month doesn't break the whole plan.

      When should I switch strategies?

      Switch when your situation changes, not when you get bored. Good reasons: your income stabilizes (variable → steady means you can commit to aggressive fixed payments), a new high-APR debt appears (that jumps the queue under any plan), or a 0% promo is about to expire.

      Also switch if you're stalling: three months of missed extra payments on an avalanche plan is a signal to grab a snowball quick win and rebuild momentum. Retake this selector whenever your answers change — the right plan at $400/month of OT is not the right plan without it.
