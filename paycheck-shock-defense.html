          Straight answers

          What if I have no buffer yet?

          Then the plan still works — the Buffer Draw just reads $0 and the gap has to come from the Hard Freeze and partial payments instead. Start the buffer with the recovery route in reverse: one micro-cut per paycheck, even $10–$25, into a separate savings account you don’t look at. The first $1,000 is the milestone that changes everything, because most real-world shocks — a cut week, a car repair, a short check — land under it.

          Which bills can actually be paused?

          Subscriptions are the easy freeze: streaming, apps, memberships — cancel or pause them today, restart later, no penalty. Utilities often allow a payment extension or arrangement if you call before the due date. Debt minimums are the dangerous pause: a missed minimum means late fees and a credit hit after 30 days, so partial beats pause there. Rent is almost never pausable — talk to your landlord early if it’s truly short; a partial rent payment with a written plan beats silence every time.

          How does the recovery math work?

          Simple division, no tricks. Whatever you drew from the buffer gets split evenly across the next 2 pay periods. The hours route divides that per-period amount by your base hourly rate — so a $160 draw at $20/hr is 8 hours total, 4 extra hours per period. The micro-cut route is the same dollars taken from spending instead of shifts. Either way, after 2 periods the buffer is back to full and your Shock Resistance Index returns to where it started.
