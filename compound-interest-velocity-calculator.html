    Quick answers

    What is compound interest?

    Compound interest is interest earned on your interest. Your balance grows, then the next round of interest is calculated on that bigger balance — so growth speeds up over time instead of staying flat. It’s the difference between money sitting still and money snowballing: the longer it compounds, the steeper the curve gets.

    HYSA vs. index funds — what’s the difference?

    A high-yield savings account (HYSA) pays a steady APY — around 4.5% in the presets above — with your principal protected and accessible anytime, which makes it a good home for an emergency fund. An index fund invests in the stock market and has historically averaged higher returns (the 8% preset), but the balance swings up and down along the way. Many people keep short-term safety money in an HYSA and invest long-term money in index funds.

    Why does starting early matter so much?

    Because compounding needs time more than it needs big deposits. Most of the growth in the chart above happens in the later years, when interest is being earned on a large balance. Starting with $50 a month at 25 can beat starting with $100 a month at 35 — every year you wait removes the most valuable compounding years at the end of the curve.
