Tier 1 · Critical

HR Advance Protocol

Your tier

Triggers when your waiting-period gap tops $1,000 and you have no short-term disability.

  • Act this week. A gap this size won't close itself — the move is a payroll emergency advance or a PTO/sick-leave cash-out from your employer, asked for before the missed check, not after.
  • Ask in writing. Use the script below — it names the exact dollar gap, the waiting period, and a repayment plan tied to your first comp check. Written requests get answered; hallway asks get forgotten.
  • Line up the fallback the same day: call your auto lender and utility company, state "workplace injury, workers' comp pending," and request a 30-day hardship deferral before any payment comes due.

Tier 2 · Moderate

Bare-Bones Injury Budget

Your tier

Triggers when comp leaves you 20% or more short of your normal take-home each week.

  • Freeze non-essentials today: streaming, subscriptions, eating out, and any autopay that isn't housing, food, utilities, transport, or a minimum payment. Every frozen dollar is a dollar the gap can't eat.
  • Pause optional debt the right way: call each card issuer, say "temporary workplace injury," and ask for a hardship program — reduced minimums or a 1–2 month deferral beat a missed payment and a late fee spiral.
  • Reallocate buffers: run the bare-bones budget in your Blueprint — it splits each comp check across essentials only until you're back on full pay.
  • Protect the essentials first if money gets tight mid-week: housing, then utilities, then food, then the car that gets you back to work.
Tier 3 · Protected

Supplemental Buffer Lock

Your tier

Triggers when your weekly loss stays under 20% of take-home — the gap is real but manageable.

  • Don't raid the emergency fund for comfort spending. Your loss is small enough to absorb with trimming — keep reserves locked for a true second emergency.
  • Watch the benefit cap: if your wage rises or OT returns before full duty, re-check that your comp check still matches 66% of your average weekly wage — underpayments are common and correctable.
  • Bank the difference on return: when full pay resumes, route the first two "catch-up" checks to rebuild whatever cushion you touched before lifestyle spending creeps back.

    Straight answers

    How long does workers' comp take to pay?

    Longer than anyone tells you at the ER. After the unpaid waiting period, most states require the carrier to issue the first check within about 14–21 days of your first missed work — but paperwork delays, employer reporting lag, and carrier reviews routinely stretch the real wait to 3–6 weeks before money is in your hand. That's the gap this page plans for: assume the slow timeline and be pleasantly surprised, not the other way around.

    What is the waiting period?

    It's the first stretch of missed work that workers' comp doesn't pay for at all — typically 3 to 7 days depending on your state (Georgia is 7 days). You lose those days of wages completely unless you cover them with PTO, sick leave, or savings. One important catch: in many states, if your disability lasts past a threshold (21 consecutive days in Georgia), the waiting-period days get paid retroactively. That refund helps later — it does nothing for rent this week.

    Can I ask HR for a payroll advance while I wait?

    Yes — and injured workers do it all the time. The strongest asks are in writing, name the exact dollar shortfall, and propose repayment from your first comp checks or cashed-out PTO. Many employers would rather advance pay than lose a trained worker to a debt spiral. Use the Tier 1 script above: it's firm, specific, and gives HR a clean way to say yes. If your employer says no, ask about cashing out accrued PTO or sick time instead — it's your earned time.

    Injury Cash Shield is an educational planning tool, not legal, tax, or financial advice, and not a substitute for your state workers' compensation board, your claims adjuster, or an attorney. Benefit rates, waiting periods, caps, and retroactive-pay rules vary by state and by claim — confirm your specifics before acting on any estimate here.
