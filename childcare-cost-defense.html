      Straight answers

      The three questions every parent paying for care asks.

      What does the Dependent Care FSA actually save me?+

      A Dependent Care FSA lets you pay for daycare, preschool, and after-school care with pre-tax dollars — up to $5,000 per household per year. Every dollar you route through it skips federal income tax and payroll tax, so at a typical combined rate around 22%, a maxed-out FSA saves roughly $1,100 a year on care you were already paying for. Two catches: it's use-it-or-lose-it (only elect what you'll truly spend), and the same dollars can't also claim the Child & Dependent Care Credit — pick the better deal per dollar, which your Blueprint above estimates for you.

      How do I stop late-pickup fees from bleeding me?+

      Late fees are usually charged per minute or per 15-minute block, and they spike when shifts run long. Three moves: (1) ask your center about an extended-hours or shift-worker plan — many charge less for a flat late window than per-incident fees; (2) build a backup pickup person into your emergency contacts so a coworker, partner, or grandparent can beat the clock; (3) budget the fees honestly — this engine already folds your average monthly penalties into the reserve target, so a bad week is pre-funded instead of a surprise. If penalties top 5% of your total care spend, treat it as a scheduling problem, not a money problem.

      When should I renegotiate my daycare rate?+

      Renegotiate when your leverage is real: at enrollment renewal, when a sibling starts, when your schedule drops a day, or when a competing center quotes less. Ask about sibling discounts (commonly 10%), prepay discounts for paying a term up front, employer or shift-worker rates, and part-time schedules priced by the day instead of the week. Centers would rather discount 10% than lose a reliable payer — especially if you can show on-time payment history. Even a $25/week reduction is $1,300 a year off your reserve target.
