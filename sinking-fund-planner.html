FAQ

Quick Answers

What is a sinking fund?

A sinking fund is money you set aside a little at a time for a specific, predictable expense you know is coming — car insurance, registration, Christmas, annual subscriptions. Instead of one big bill wrecking a paycheck, you split it across every paycheck between now and the due date. The expense stops being an emergency because you funded it on purpose, in advance.

Sinking fund vs. emergency fund — what's the difference?

An emergency fund is for expenses you can't predict: a blown transmission, a cut shift, a medical surprise. A sinking fund is for expenses you can predict — you know the amount and roughly when it's due. Mixing the two is how emergency funds quietly drain away on normal bills. Keep them separate: sinking funds handle the known bills so the emergency fund survives for actual emergencies.

What if a bill is due next week?

Then the math gets blunt: with only one paycheck left, the "set-aside" is basically the whole bill. This planner will show you that number honestly rather than pretend a small deposit covers it. Your options: pay it from savings or the emergency fund this once, ask the provider about a payment plan or a due-date move, and — most important — restart the fund the day after you pay it, so next time you get the full runway of paychecks. A short runway is exactly what the stress check above is warning about.
