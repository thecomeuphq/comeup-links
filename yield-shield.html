Straight answers

Is my money safe in an online bank? +
Yes — as long as the bank is FDIC-insured (or the credit union is NCUA-insured). Your deposits are protected up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank — the exact same federal protection a big branch bank offers. Before opening any account, verify the bank on the FDIC's BankFind tool and make sure you're on the bank's real website, not a lookalike.
How fast can I get my money in a real emergency? +
Transfers from an online high-yield account to your linked checking account typically take 1–2 business days. That's why the smart setup is a split: keep a same-day buffer (about one month of essentials) in checking for true right-now emergencies, and park the rest where it earns. Some online banks also offer same-day or instant transfers for a small fee — check before you need it.
What APY is actually realistic right now? +
Regular big-bank savings still sits around 0.01% APY, while competitive online high-yield savings accounts have recently offered roughly 3.75%–4.25% APY. These rates are variable — they move with the Federal Reserve's rate decisions and can change at any time, up or down. Use the slider above with a rate you can verify on a bank's site today rather than trusting any number printed anywhere, including here.
Will moving my savings hurt my credit or my main bank account? +
No. Opening a savings account is not a loan application — most high-yield accounts use a soft identity check that doesn't touch your credit score. You keep your existing checking account, direct deposit, and bill pay exactly as they are; the HYSA just sits alongside them, linked for transfers.
